What moves re-suppression — adjusted odds ratios

Each factor's adjusted odds ratio (AOR) from one logistic model, holding the others constant. Right of the line (AOR > 1) is more likely to re-suppress; left is less. A whisker that crosses 1 is not statistically significant.

Every variable, univariate and adjusted

Group re-suppression rate, unadjusted odds ratio, and adjusted OR with 95% CI. The per-variable test is chi-square (categorical), a Cochran–Armitage trend (ordered), or Mann–Whitney (continuous). Reference level in each block is odds 1.

Spec §7.2

Mortality in the unsuppressed cohort

2024 Nigerian National Guidelines for HIV Prevention, Treatment and Care

Enhanced Adherence Counselling

Why it matters

EAC is a critical component of the HIV continuum of care. It ensures that diagnosis is followed by retention and sustained virological suppression, thereby reducing morbidity, mortality, and transmission risk. It proactively addresses adherence challenges to prevent treatment failure, HIV drug resistance, and opportunistic infections.

EAC is a mandatory intervention before any clinical decision to switch ART regimens.

Definition

An intensive, individualised adherence support intervention for non-stable recipients of care who have adherence issues, poor treatment response, or clinical or virological signs of treatment failure.

Who needs EAC

Any one of the following:

≥ 1,000
Unsuppressed viral load (copies/ml)
50 – 999
Suppressed but detectable — low-level viraemia
WHO 3–4
Clinical failure: new or recurrent Stage 3/4 after ≥6 months on effective ART

The four-step process

  1. Structured assessment — formal evaluation of current ART adherence.
  2. Barrier exploration using the ABCDE framework.
  3. Collaborative identification of solutions.
  4. Joint development of an individualised adherence intervention plan.

The ABCDE framework

AAdherence problems — missed doses, timing, and other lapses.
BBugs — intercurrent or opportunistic infections.
CCorrect ART dosage — especially weight-based dosing in children.
DDrug–drug interactions.
ErEsistance — potential HIV drug resistance.

Duration and modality

A minimum of 3 structured sessions over a 3-month period. For clients with low-level viraemia (50–999 copies/ml), sessions may be delivered in person or virtually (telephone or similar).

Expected outcomes and monitoring

Repeat viral load is taken only after 3 consecutive months of documented good adherence (more than 95% of doses taken) following EAC completion.

If the repeat VL remains ≥ 1,000 copies/ml, a multidisciplinary switch committee is required — doctor, nurse, and adherence counsellor.

Managing persistent low-level viraemia

  • 200–999 copies/ml — high risk of treatment failure. Recommend switching to second-line.
  • 50–199 copies/ml — high likelihood of re-suppression. Continue frequent monitoring.

Documentation

Use the standard PMM tools: the EAC Form and the EAC Monitoring Register.

Data Analysis Plan v3.0

The unit of analysis is a failure episode

A client may fail, complete EAC, re-suppress, and fail again. That is two index events and two trips through the cascade — one client, two episodes. The primary key is (S/N, index VL date, index VL value), never S/N alone.

De-duplicating on S/N would delete 294 second failures — the highest-priority switch candidates in the programme.

Rates are computed per episode. Distinct client counts are shown alongside so no headline double-counts a person.

The index cohort is an open cohort with quarterly enrolment

A snapshot of clients whose current VL is ≥ 1,000, drawn on the same day as the line list, cannot support the outcome half of the cascade. A post-EAC VL becomes the current VL, so a client who re-suppresses leaves the cohort, and one who is still failing has no later result. Steps 7–10 compute to exactly zero.

Building the index list quarterly freezes each index event at the quarter it occurred, so a post-EAC VL can land after it. That is why the cascade has an outcome arm at all.
